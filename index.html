How do everyday technologies shape aging, independence and social inclusion?

I am interested in how technologies such as care robots, smart-home applications and generative AI are imagined and adopted in everyday life, and whether they reduce or reinforce existing inequalities.

Technology in everyday life

I am interested in how people encounter and use everyday technologies, from smart-home applications to generative AI, and how these technologies affect daily routines, relationships and independence. My smart-home app review analysis and Korean-language writing on generative AI have given me different ways to examine these questions. Watching Robot & Frank (2012) and comparing AI-generated and human translations during a Digital Humanities project in the first half of 2026 also prompted me to reflect on the relationship between humans and robots. AI produced translations quickly, but human translation was more helpful for understanding the content, context and nuance. This experience made me consider the importance of human interpretation and understanding alongside technological efficiency.

Aging, care and independence

My interest in aging and technology has grown out of family experiences, including caring for my grandmother and helping my mother use digital tools. These experiences have led me to consider what roles care robots and AI might play in older adults’ daily lives, and how technological support relates to autonomy, social connection and human care.

Social inclusion and measurement

My family’s experiences with medical information and healthcare decisions have made me attentive to differences in information access and health literacy. My social work training has also shaped how I think about service access and individual needs. In my master’s thesis, I examined life satisfaction among middle-aged and older retirees using panel data, and I am interested in how quantitative and text-based methods can help describe inequalities in later life.

These interests draw on my background in social research, quantitative analysis, computational text analysis and iOS development. I would like to develop a stronger research foundation for studying technology and aging.
